zhanna_text: >-
  This was such an exciting find! A few weeks ago, I heard that the lake at Lackawanna State Park would be drawn down to allow for construction on the dam. I knew that at least part of the old Route 407 remained beneath the lake, but I wasn't aware that it was intact, including the old bridge that used to cross the South Branch of Tunkhannock Creek, until I started seeing the photos people were posting of the area, post-drawdown. Of course, my first thought was, could this bridge have held a benchmark, possibly an old PDH mark? It was certainly possible, and it meant almost nothing that there was no record of such a mark, because many of the old PDH marks we've found are not documented anywhere that we're aware of.

  Today, on a gorgeous Sunday in the middle of a string of dry, sunny days unlike anything we saw the rest of this year, Rich and I took a drive over to the lake to check it out. It was like wandering around on another planet, and we spent a lot longer here than I expected we would. Interesting sights were the fish habitats strewn all around the lake bed, the *three* bridges (not just two; a third, likely much older, stone bridge was also revealed when the water receded), and the "quick mud" that claimed a few locals as victims (not permanently). But the star of the show for us was, of course, the PDH elevation mark that we found on the southwestern wingwall of the old Route 407 bridge! It was such a treat to discover that there is actually a mark here, and that it may not be seen again for many decades. 

  The mark is in good condition (as you might expect). This may be the first time I've seen an ampersand stamped into a disk! I wonder what the designation refers to. It's even in a different format from others I've seen.
